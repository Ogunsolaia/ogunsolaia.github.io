Academic Profile

I am a doctoral researcher in Statistics at the University of Manchester, specialising in spatial causal inference, statistical methodology, and applied statistical modelling.

My research focuses on developing methods for causal effect estimation from observational data in the presence of spatial confounding and interference. My broader research interests include spatiotemporal statistics, probability distribution theory, environmental health, and reproducible statistical computing.

I have university teaching experience in the United Kingdom and Nigeria and hold Associate Fellowship of Advance HE (AFHEA). I am also a developer and co-author of the open-source R package spaci.

Research Contributions

My doctoral research investigates the joint consequences of spatial confounding and interference for causal effect estimation.

My methodological contributions include:

  • Analytical investigation and decomposition of biases arising from spatial confounding and interference.
  • Development of iDAPS, a matching-based approach incorporating propensity score similarity, geographical proximity, and neighbourhood treatment exposure.
  • Development of RecoverU+, an approach incorporating spatial confounder recovery and neighbourhood exposure within a causal estimation framework.
  • Development and computational implementation of a unified framework for estimating direct causal effects under spatial confounding and interference.

My broader research includes spatiotemporal modelling of road traffic crashes, statistical distribution development, and applications of statistical methods to health and socioeconomic data.
