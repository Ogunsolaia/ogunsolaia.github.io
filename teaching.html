Teaching

Statistics at Higher Education Level

Teaching Overview

I am an Associate Fellow of Advance HE (AFHEA) with teaching experience in mathematics, statistics, and statistical computing across higher education institutions in the United Kingdom and Nigeria.

I currently work as a Graduate Teaching Assistant in the Department of Mathematics at the University of Manchester and Lecturer in the Department of Statistics at the Federal University of Agriculture, Abeokuta, Nigeria.

My teaching experience spans foundation-year and undergraduate mathematics, probability theory, statistical inference, regression analysis, generalised linear models, experimental design, and statistical computing.

I am particularly interested in helping students develop mathematical understanding, statistical reasoning, computational competence, and the confidence to apply these skills to real-world problems.

Teaching Philosophy

My approach to teaching is centred on making statistics accessible, engaging, and relevant to students with different academic backgrounds and levels of confidence.

I believe that effective statistical education should extend beyond mathematical derivations and computational procedures. Students should understand the reasoning behind statistical methods, the assumptions on which they depend, and the interpretation of their results.

My teaching approach is guided by four principles:

  • Conceptual understanding: Helping students understand the underlying mathematical and statistical ideas rather than relying solely on memorisation.
  • Active learning: Encouraging participation, problem-solving, independent thinking, and discussion.
  • Practical application: Connecting theoretical concepts to real-world examples and computational exercises.
  • Inclusive learning: Recognising differences in students’ prior knowledge, learning needs, and confidence, while providing appropriate academic support and constructive feedback.

I aim to create a supportive learning environment in which students feel comfortable asking questions, exploring alternative solutions, and developing their analytical skills.

Statistical Computing and Practical Education

I consider statistical computing an important component of modern mathematics and statistics education.

My teaching experience includes supporting students in the use of statistical software for data management, exploratory data analysis, statistical modelling, hypothesis testing, visualisation, and interpretation of results.

I have experience teaching and supporting statistical analysis using:

  • R and RStudio.
  • IBM SPSS Statistics.
  • Microsoft Excel.

I am particularly interested in integrating reproducible statistical computing into teaching, helping students connect mathematical theory with practical data analysis.

My research experience in developing statistical methods and open-source software also informs my approach to computational statistics education.
